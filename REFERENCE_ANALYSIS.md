# Reference analysis and AERG adaptation

## Reference structure (observed)
- Home: introductory mission, symbolic identity, philosophy, five giving pillars, collaboration invitation.
- About: trust overview, vision/mission, board biographies, financial-disclosure section.
- Work: focus-area overview, partner profiles, project descriptions and reported/expected impact.
- Blog: featured personal reflection and story invitation.
- Contact: direct email, focus geography and LinkedIn.
- Shared interface: sticky navigation, responsive layout, footer navigation and repeated contact calls to action.
- Styling inspected: cool off-white, indigo and cyan; Outfit headings, Inter body; rounded buttons/cards, subtle gradients, spacious sections.

## AERG implementation
The architecture is adapted to a restoration group rather than a grantmaking trust. Identity, palette, logo illustration and editorial copy are original. Forest green, warm cream and lime communicate an ecological direction. A large forest visual introduces the site, followed by connected focus areas, a proposed working approach and editorial cards.

## Page mapping
Home → ecosystem-restoration introduction, connected-system focus cards, four-step proposed approach.
About → proposed vision, mission, values and a governance publication checklist.
Work → five proposed focus areas, category filters and dedicated detail views. No fabricated project or impact records.
Blog → The Living Journal, containing three original sample articles clearly marked for review.
Contact → validated local enquiry-draft download, frequently asked questions, explicit backend limitation.
Additional → draft Privacy page and text-edit/export controls.

## Important distinctions
This is not an exact visual duplicate and does not reuse the reference organisation’s logo, board, partner names, project evidence or charitable status. Proposed AERG content is labelled as draft or concept. The user supplied the organisation name only; all other organisation-specific claims require approval. The website is a complete navigable front-end prototype, not a production service or a deployed site.

## Needed for launch
Approved logo and brand direction; organisation/legal identity; team bios; actual project and programme content; evidence-backed impact information; email, location and social links; hosting/domain; real form delivery; approved privacy and other required policies.
